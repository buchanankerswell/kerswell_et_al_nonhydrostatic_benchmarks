# Feedback formulation

Written 2026-11-26 on `agent/formulation-audit`. This note explains the pressure-wave feedback, compares it with ASPECT's two-phase melt transport, and audits the two bulk-viscosity damping terms. It records findings only; no code or PRM files changed. Notation follows `kerswell_et_al_660_kinetics/draft/manuscript.md`, including `eq:` labels. Where the manuscript and ASPECT use the same symbol for different things I say so.

## The loop

The governing equations are Stokes, $\nabla P - \nabla\cdot\sigma^{\prime} = \rho\,g$ (`eq:navier-stokes-no-inertia`), and the projected-density continuity equation, $\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot\vec{u} + \left(\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla\rho\right)\cdot\vec{u} = 0$ (`eq:continuity-expanded`). ASPECT's single-phase stress is deviatoric, $\sigma^{\prime} = 2\eta\dot{\epsilon}^{\prime}$, so the bulk viscosity is zero: pressure is the only isotropic stress. Density comes from a linearized equation of state, $\rho \approx \bar{\rho}(1 + \bar{\beta}\hat{P} - \bar{\alpha}\hat{T})$ (`eq:density-ala`) plus the phase jump $\Delta\rho\,X$, with $\hat{P} = P - \bar{P}$. The reaction driving force is $\Delta G \approx \Delta\bar{G} + \hat{P}\Delta\bar{V} - \hat{T}\Delta\bar{S}$ (`eq:gibbs`), and $\dot{X}$ follows `eq:reaction-rate-410` or `eq:reaction-rate-660`.

Five steps close the loop. A perturbation $\hat{P}$ changes $\Delta G$ by $\hat{P}\Delta\bar{V}$ and so changes $\dot{X}$. The reaction changes $X$ and therefore $\rho$ by $\Delta\rho\,\dot{X}$. Continuity then demands $\nabla\cdot\vec{u} \simeq -\bar{\beta}\,\partial\hat{P}/\partial t - (\Delta\rho/\bar{\rho})\,\dot{X} - (\nabla\rho/\rho)\cdot\vec{u}$. Stokes converts that divergence into a pressure, $\hat{P} \sim M\,\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$, where $M = 4\eta/3 + \zeta$ is the longitudinal viscosity for a compressional mode. Back to the first step. At 14.14 GPa the olivine-wadsleyite table gives $\Delta\bar{V} = -2.07\times10^{-6}$ $\mathrm{m}^3\,\mathrm{mol}^{-1}$ and $\Delta\rho = 182$ $\mathrm{kg}\,\mathrm{m}^{-3}$ (`simulation/data/olivine-wadsleyite-profile-Mg100.tsv`); the reaction-chain header confirms $\Delta G < 0$ favours the denser product (`cahn1956_interface_controlled_growth.h:67-69`), so a positive $\hat{P}$ speeds the forward reaction.

Two code facts make the loop fast. ASPECT solves for the full pressure by default (`doc/sphinx/user/methods/pressure-static-dyn.md:59`), and the plugin uses `in.pressure[q]` as the driving pressure (`simulation/plugins/nh-kinetics.cc:149,174-177`). The density field is reset each step from `out.densities`, which itself depends on $\hat{P}$ (`nh-kinetics.cc:280-284,371-384`); `drho_dt` is a finite difference of that field (`aspect/source/simulator/assemblers/stokes.cc:815-834`). The $\bar{\beta}\,\partial\hat{P}/\partial t$ part of continuity is therefore explicit and one step behind the Stokes solve. `benchmarks/compressibility_formulations/lateral_pipe_increase_pressure/` separates exactly this: the two PRMs differ only in `Use adiabatic pressure for density` (true versus false), and the plugin comment says the switch exists "to prevent pressure waves from forming" (`plugins/compressibility_formulations.cc:128-133`).

The continuum loop is stable. Taking a short-wavelength mode and combining $\hat{P} = M\,\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$ with continuity gives $M\bar{\beta}\,\partial\hat{P}/\partial t + (1 + M\gamma\kappa)\hat{P} = \hat{P}_\mathrm{buoy} - M\gamma\dot{X}_0$, where $\gamma = \Delta\rho/\bar{\rho}$ and $\kappa = \partial\dot{X}/\partial\hat{P} > 0$. The decay rate is $1/(M\bar{\beta}) + \gamma\kappa/\bar{\beta}$, so raising $M$ also removes viscous damping. What oscillates is the **discrete** loop: writing $p$ for $\hat{P}$, with the lagged density and reaction terms, $p^n = -(a+b)\,p^{n-1} + a\,p^{n-2}$, with $a = M\bar{\beta}/\Delta t$ and $b = M\gamma\kappa$. The growing root alternates in sign, so the artifact is a sign-flipping pressure wave; instability needs $a+b \gtrsim 1$. Order-of-magnitude values from the suite put it near marginal, which fits the measured sensitivity: Res5 is clean, Res6 oscillates, Res7 crashes; CFL1.0 is clean, CFL0.25 crashes (`notes/key-results.md:15`). This derivation is a local, short-wavelength approximation and still needs a numerical check.

## Why a bulk viscosity cannot damp it

Divergence is not free. Continuity fixes $\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$ to accommodate $\partial\rho/\partial t$ and the reaction volume change. A bulk viscosity adds $\zeta(\nabla\cdot\vec{u})\mathbf{I}$ to the stress, so momentum becomes $\nabla P - \nabla\cdot(2\eta\dot{\epsilon}^{\prime}) - \nabla(\zeta\nabla\cdot\vec{u}) = \rho g$. Penalising a divergence that continuity already prescribes does not reduce it; the penalty appears as a larger pressure gradient. Because $M$ grows with $\zeta$, both $a$ and $b$ grow, and the alternating root grows with them. The steady pressure produced by a given reaction rate also scales with $M$, and $\hat{P}$ is the quantity that drives $\dot{X}$. The loop gain rises with $\zeta$.

The measurements follow that prediction. Peak short-wavelength amplitude goes 157 MPa (no damping), 165 MPa (`Bimpl1e18`), 181 MPa (`Bimpl1e19`), 225 MPa (`Bimpl1e20`); `Bexpl1e19` gives 211 MPa and `Bexpl1e20` crashes at step 1 (`simulation/results/relaxed-mean-check.csv`). The largest pressure range goes 2168, 2173, 2226, 2530 MPa in the same order (`simulation/results/pressure-history-summary.csv`). No bulk run shows a positive reduction against the undamped control (`notes/key-results.md:9`).

## What the two-phase melt models do

ASPECT's melt implementation (Keller et al. 2013, `10.1093/gji/ggt306`; Dannberg & Heister 2016, `10.1093/gji/ggw329`) solves for solid velocity $\vec{u}_s$, fluid pressure $p_f$, and a third unknown, the compaction pressure $p_c$. I write melt porosity as $\varphi$; the ASPECT manual calls it $\phi$, which the manuscript already uses for equilibrium phase fraction.

$$-\nabla\cdot\left[2\eta\left(\dot{\epsilon}(\vec{u}_s) - \tfrac{1}{3}(\nabla\cdot\vec{u}_s)\mathbf{1}\right)\right] + \nabla p_f + \nabla p_c = \rho\,\vec{g}$$

$$\nabla\cdot\vec{u}_s - \nabla\cdot K_D\nabla p_f = -\nabla\cdot(K_D\rho_f\vec{g}) + \Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\rho_f}-\frac{1}{\rho_s}\right) + \ldots \qquad \nabla\cdot\vec{u}_s + \frac{p_c}{\xi} = 0$$

with $K_D = k/\eta_f$, melt velocity $\vec{u}_f = \vec{u}_s - (K_D/\varphi)(\nabla p_f - \rho_f\vec{g})$, and $p_c = (1-\varphi)(p_s-p_f) = -\xi\nabla\cdot\vec{u}_s$ (`doc/sphinx/user/methods/melt-transport.md:18-27,28-58,60-72,120-125`). The porosity equation is $\partial\varphi/\partial t + \vec{u}_s\cdot\nabla\varphi = \Gamma/\rho_s + (1-\varphi)(\nabla\cdot\vec{u}_s + \beta_s\rho_s\vec{g}\cdot\vec{u}_s)$ (`melt-transport.md:154-174`).

The difference from our $\zeta\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$ is structural. In the melt assembler $\xi$ appears only in the $p_c$–$p_c$ block and the two $p_c$–divergence couplings (`source/simulator/melt.cc:530-551`); eliminating $p_c$ recovers an effective $\xi(\nabla\cdot\vec{v},\nabla\cdot\vec{u})$ term, so the continuum operator matches a single-phase bulk viscosity, but $p_c$ remains an independent finite-element variable with its own equation, scaling (`p_c_scale`) and Schur-complement preconditioner that includes $1/\xi$ (`melt.cc:247-291`). Our `Bimpl` adds $\zeta$ straight to the velocity–velocity block with no extra variable and no preconditioner change. In melt the compaction pressure takes up the divergence; in ours only one pressure exists, so the resistance shows up in $\hat{P}$, which drives the reaction.

Key parameters: $k = k_0\varphi^3(1-\varphi)^2$ with $k_0 = 10^{-8}$ $\mathrm{m}^2$; $\xi = \xi_0\varphi_0/\varphi$ with $\xi_0 = 10^{22}$ Pa s, $\varphi_0 = 0.05$; $\eta \approx 5\times10^{20}$ Pa s; $\eta_f = 10$ Pa s; $\rho_s-\rho_f \approx 500$ $\mathrm{kg}\,\mathrm{m}^{-3}$; $\Gamma/\rho_s = \Delta\varphi/\text{melting time scale}$ with the scale at least one reaction substep (default $10^3$ yr); $\varphi$ clamped to $[10^{-4},0.995]$; compaction length $\delta = \sqrt{(\xi+4\eta/3)K_D}$ (`source/material_model/reaction_model/katz2003_mantle_melting.cc:291-341,450-460,526-542`; `doc/sphinx/parameters/Mesh_20refinement.md:154`; the refinement code at `source/mesh_refinement/compaction_length.cc:72-73` swaps $\eta$ and $\xi$, a doc-code mismatch). At $\xi\to0$ the $1/\xi$ block diverges and $p_c\to0$; at $\xi\to\infty$ the divergence is forced to zero and $p_c$ degenerates to a Lagrange multiplier. Neither limit is used in shipped models.

**Zero permeability does not give our formulation.** With $K_D\to0$ the Darcy flux vanishes and $\vec{u}_f\to\vec{u}_s$, but $p_f$ and $p_c$ remain unknowns, the $p_c$ block scales toward zero, and the system becomes singular rather than reducing. In the code the limit is a model switch: cells below $K_D$ threshold are marked non-melt, every $p_c$ degree of freedom is constrained to zero, and all melt terms vanish (`source/simulator/melt.cc:1616-1626,1644-1649`). That gives plain single-phase Stokes with **no** bulk viscosity and no $\Gamma$. What survives in the equations is the momentum and continuity pair; what disappears is the compaction pressure, the segregation flux, the porosity source, and the density-rate coupling. Our formulation instead has one pressure, an explicit $\partial\rho/\partial t$ sourced by $\hat{P}$ and $\dot{X}$, and a reaction rate driven by $\hat{P}$.

The melt models are stable for five reasons that have nothing to do with damping $\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$. Their reaction rate $\Gamma$ is computed from temperature and `get_adiabatic_conditions().pressure()` — the static adiabat, not the Stokes or compaction pressure (`katz2003_mantle_melting.cc:189,197,209`; `source/material_model/melt_global.cc:186-188`); ASPECT's own parameter documentation warns that full pressure in reaction rates is unstable when compaction pressure is present (`doc/sphinx/parameters/Material_20model.md:4180`). Their mass conservation is isentropic compression or incompressible, never `projected density field` (`source/simulator/melt.cc:1738-1743`), so no explicit $\beta\partial p/\partial t$ source exists. $\Gamma$ is rate-limited by a melting time scale and integrated by operator splitting with at least ten substeps (`melt_simple.cc:62-66`; `katz2003_mantle_melting.cc:609-623`). The $(1-\varphi)\nabla\cdot\vec{u}_s$ term is implicit in $\varphi$, with SUPG and porosity limiters (`melt.cc:823-837,879-892`). Finally the three-field Stokes system has a matching preconditioner. Remove the pressure-rate coupling from $\Gamma$ and there is no loop for $\xi$ to amplify.

## What we implemented

All damping kinds are set in `project.mk:306-320` and `simulation/params/templates/NH/shared/{solver,material}.prm`.

| Kind | Parameter | Where the term lands |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| `DynpOn` / `DynpOff` | `Use dynamic pressure correction for density` and `... for gibbs` | Both flags follow the same switch (`project.mk:306-307`): `DynpOff` removes $\hat{P}$ from $\rho$ **and** from $\Delta G$ (`nh-kinetics.cc:282,354`) |
| `Bimpl` | `Enable bulk viscosity`, `Constant bulk viscosity` (Pa s) | $+\zeta(\nabla\cdot\vec{v},\nabla\cdot\vec{u})$ in the Stokes velocity block (`aspect` branch `agent/bulk-viscosity-chain`, `source/simulator/assemblers/stokes.cc:561-611`) |
| `Bexpl` | `Enable additional Stokes RHS`, `Explicit bulk viscosity` (Pa s) | $rhs_p[q] = (\zeta/\eta)\,\mathrm{div}\,u$ (`nh-kinetics.cc:183-195`), i.e. the continuity RHS |
| `Rel` | `Relaxation time for dynamic pressure` (s) | Prognostic `relaxed_pressure` field relaxed toward `in.pressure` (`nh-kinetics.cc:375-379`) |
| `Cap` | `Maximum dynamic pressure correction` (Pa) | Clamp on $\hat{P}$ (`nh-kinetics.cc:178-181`) |

I write $\tau_p$ for the relaxation time; the manuscript reserves $\tau$ for the reaction completion timescale (`eq:reaction-timescale`). `DynpOff` is the no-feedback reference, but it is a **double** control: it removes $\hat{P}$ from $\Delta G$ and from density. The manuscript's stabilization removes only $\hat{P}\Delta\bar{V}$ from $\Delta G$ and retains $\hat{P}$ in density (`manuscript.md:259-261`), so the suite has no run that isolates the kinetic coupling alone. The ASPECT melt documentation makes the same recommendation about reaction rates.

## Bugs

Confirmed from code, highest impact first.

1. **Bexpl uses the wrong equation.** `rhs_p` is documented as the force for the conservation-of-mass equation (`aspect/include/aspect/material_model/interface.h:1228-1232`) and enters the pressure row with a plus sign (`source/simulator/assemblers/stokes.cc:398`). The assembled continuity equation is $-\nabla\cdot\vec{u} = (1/\rho)D\rho/Dt + \mathrm{rhs}_p$. A bulk viscosity is a viscous stress in momentum, not a mass source. The term injects or removes mass in proportion to divergence. Critical.
2. **Bexpl has the wrong sign and the wrong units.** $rhs_p = +(\zeta/\eta)\,\mathrm{div}\,u_\mathrm{old}$ makes the correction alternate in sign: $\nabla\cdot\vec{u}_\mathrm{new} \approx -(\zeta/\eta)\nabla\cdot\vec{u}_\mathrm{old}$. It adds $\zeta/\eta$ to the alternating-mode gain. The parameter is declared Pa s but dividing by $\eta$ (Pa s) leaves a dimensionless coefficient, so no stress $\zeta\,\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$ (Pa) ever appears. $\zeta/\eta$ is 0.01 at `1e19` and 0.1 at `1e20`, too small to damp anything and large enough to destabilise. Critical.
3. **Bexpl is lagged.** `in.strain_rate` comes from `current_linearization_point` (`source/simulator/assembly.cc:655`), the previous velocity under `single Advection, single Stokes`. A one-step-old divergence driving the current continuity equation is the classic route to alternating grid-scale oscillation; it fits `Bexpl1e20` crashing at step 1 and the CFL0.25 failures. High.
4. **Bimpl omits $\zeta$ from the preconditioner and Newton assembler.** `StokesBulkViscosityTerm` is registered only in `assemblers->stokes_system` (`source/simulator/assembly.cc:81-83`); the commit message states the preconditioner and Newton assembler are unchanged. `StokesPreconditioner` and `StokesCompressiblePreconditioner` still build $2\eta\dot{\epsilon}:\dot{\epsilon} - (2\eta/3)(\nabla\cdot\vec{v},\nabla\cdot\vec{u})$ and the unchanged $M_p/\eta$ Schur approximation (`stokes.cc:124-127,238-277`). The melt preconditioner explicitly includes $1/\xi$. With $\zeta = 10^{20}$ Pa s and the viscosity floor at $10^{19}$ Pa s, the divergence part of the true A block can be ten times the preconditioner's in the softest cells. High.
5. **`BulkViscosityOutputs::average()` and `AdditionalMaterialOutputsStokesRHS::average()` are no-op TODOs** (`aspect/include/aspect/material_model/interface.h:1212-1219,1258-1261`). Harmless today because no PRM sets material averaging, but either damping term would behave inconsistently if it did. Medium.
6. **`Bexpl` diagnostics are unreliable.** The Stokes-RHS output is created whenever `Explicit bulk viscosity > 0`, including postprocessing evaluations where `in.strain_rate` is `signaling_nan` and the code silently substitutes zero (`nh-kinetics.cc:187-192,594-600`). Medium.

Two things that are **not** bugs: `Bimpl`'s sign and factor are correct (the compressible correction already supplies $-2\eta/3$, so $+\zeta$ gives the right longitudinal viscosity), and the two formulations are never enabled together (`project.mk:308-320`). The plugin's `Bexpl` gate `in.requests_property(additional_outputs)` is always satisfied in the Stokes assembly (`assembly.cc:656-660`). Rates and relaxation times are consistently SI; there is no year-to-second error.

## Unphysical math

1. **A bulk viscosity damps a quantity that continuity controls.** $\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$ is set by $\partial\rho/\partial t$ and $\Delta\rho\,\dot{X}$, not by viscous flow. Raising $\zeta$ raises the pressure needed to produce that mandated divergence, and $\hat{P}$ is the feedback variable. The expectation that damping $\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$ damps the oscillation reverses cause and effect.
2. **A phase transformation is not a viscous volume strain.** The volume change is a change of reference density at fixed mass; the correct representation is the continuity source $-(\Delta\rho/\rho)\dot{X}$, which the projected-density formulation already has. In melt transport $\xi$ is physical because a porous solid skeleton really deforms to change $\varphi$, and a second phase has its own pressure. Our single-phase system has no skeleton and no second pressure. The physically relevant damping parameter is a reaction or volume-change relaxation time, not a mechanical $\zeta$. The manuscript's own caveat says $\Delta G$ should use the mean normal stress actually experienced by the rock (`manuscript.md:380`); adding $\zeta$ makes the finite-element pressure differ from that stress by $\zeta\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$, and the plugin does not correct for it.
3. **`Bexpl` violates mass conservation by construction.** A source proportional to $\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$ in the continuity equation rescales compressibility rather than adding a stress.
4. **The equation of state enters twice, once through an explicit lag.** $\rho(\hat{P})$ is evaluated in the material model and then differenced into `drho_dt` in continuity. The $\bar{\beta}\partial\hat{P}/\partial t$ part should be an implicit pressure–mass term; ASPECT's `isentropic_compression` and `implicit_reference_density_profile` formulations treat it differently, while `projected density field` leaves it explicit. This is the root cause the damping terms do not touch.
5. **$\zeta$ acts on all divergence modes.** `Bimpl` has no selector for the reaction-driven part, so it stiffens hydrostatic and elastic divergence along with the kinetic signal.

## What works and why

`Rel` and `Cap` reduce the kinetic feedback gain $\gamma\kappa$ instead of raising $M$. Filtering the driving pressure with $\tau_p \ge 1$ kyr cuts short-wavelength amplitude to 0.3–7 MPa and the largest range to 408–426 MPa; `Cap1e8` gives 354 MPa; `DynpOff` cuts both couplings and gives 386 MPa and 0.4 MPa (`notes/key-results.md:7,11,13`). `Rel` and `Cap` act on $\hat{P}$ before it reaches $\Delta G$; `DynpOff` also removes the $\bar{\beta}\partial\hat{P}/\partial t$ part of continuity.

## Potential solutions

1. Break the loop at the reaction driving force: compute $\Delta G$ from $\bar{P}$ or from a relaxed field, and keep $\hat{P}$ in density. This is the manuscript's stabilization and the ASPECT melt practice; our `DynpOff` goes further and also removes $\hat{P}$ from density. Add a run with `dynp-gibbs=false` and `dynp-density=true` to isolate the kinetic coupling, and test whether the reference pressure used in `projected_density.prm` removes the wave on its own.
2. Rate-limit the volume change. Give $\dot{X}$ a finite relaxation time at least as long as the advection timestep and integrate it with operator splitting, as melt does with the melting time scale. This attacks the chemical source rather than the mechanical stress.
3. Make $\bar{\beta}\partial\hat{P}/\partial t$ implicit. Add a pressure–mass matrix term $(\bar{\beta}/\Delta t)(q,p)$ or compare against `implicit_reference_density_profile` and `isentropic_compression` control runs.
4. If $\zeta$ stays, rebuild it as the melt formulation does: introduce $p_c$ as a separate variable with $\nabla\cdot\vec{u} + p_c/\zeta = 0$, add $\zeta$ to the preconditioner and Newton assembler, reconstruct the mechanical pressure for $\Delta G$, and validate against `benchmarks/solitary_wave/`.
5. Delete the `Bexpl` `rhs_p` term outright. Fixing its sign would still leave a mass source, not a viscous stress. If an explicit stress is wanted, add $-(\zeta\nabla\cdot\vec{u}_\mathrm{old},\nabla\cdot\vec{v})$ to the momentum RHS with $\zeta$ in Pa s.
6. Add a per-step diagnostic for `rhs_p`, $\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$, $\hat{P}$, `relaxed_pressure`, and `drho_dt`. The current suite cannot separate a physical load from a field bias.

## Open questions

**The ~100 MPa relaxed offset is not a filter lag.** All seven relaxed runs settle near $-135$ MPa mean dynamic pressure against $-37$ MPa for `DynpOff` and $-57$ MPa for the undamped case, and the offset spans only 96.6–100.8 MPa while $\tau_p$ spans four orders of magnitude, 10 yr to 100 kyr (`simulation/results/relaxed-mean-check.csv`). A lag proportional to $\tau_p$ cannot do that. Two candidates worth testing: `simulation/params/templates/NH/sink/initial.prm:8` initializes `relaxed_pressure` to $2.0\times10^9 + 3900\,g\,(10^6-y)$, a fixed-density lithostat that differs from the ambient-corrected adiabat used for $\bar{P}$; and the field is an advected tracer relaxed toward **total** pressure, whose steady state is $p_\mathrm{rel} = P - \tau_p\,D p_\mathrm{rel}/Dt$, not $P$. Write the field and the Stokes pressure each step (open item 1 in `notes/key-results.md:23`), check the compositional-field boundary conditions, and consider initializing from $\bar{P}$ and relaxing toward $\hat{P}$.

The linearized recursion above needs a numerical check against the pipe benchmark before it is used as a design rule. The mechanical-versus-thermodynamic pressure question raised under unphysical math also needs a decision: it changes which pressure $\Delta G$ should see once a bulk viscosity exists.
