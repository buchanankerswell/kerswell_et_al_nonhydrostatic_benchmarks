## Findings, in `notes/feedback-formulation.md`

The loop closes through five steps: a perturbation `$\hat{P}$` changes `$\Delta G$` by `$\hat{P}\Delta\bar{V}$` and so changes `$\dot{X}$`; the reaction changes `$X$` and density by `$\Delta\rho\,\dot{X}$`; continuity then fixes `$\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$`; Stokes converts that divergence into pressure; back to the first step. ASPECT solves for full pressure and the plugin drives density and Gibbs energy from it, so the density field prescribed each step depends on `$\hat{P}$` and its finite difference `drho_dt` is one step behind the Stokes solve. The continuum loop is stable. What oscillates is the discrete, lagged loop `$p^n = -(a+b)\,p^{n-1} + a\,p^{n-2}$` with `$a = M\bar{\beta}/\Delta t$`, `$b = M\gamma\kappa$`, `$M = 4\eta/3 + \zeta$`; the growing root alternates in sign.

A bulk viscosity cannot damp this because continuity controls `$\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$`, not viscous flow. Penalising a mandated divergence shows up as a larger pressure gradient, and `$\hat{P}$` is the feedback variable, so the loop gain rises with `$\zeta$`. Measured short-wavelength amplitude goes 157, 165, 181, 225 MPa as Bimpl `$\zeta$` goes 0, 1e18, 1e19, 1e20; `Bexpl1e19` gives 211 MPa and `Bexpl1e20` crashes at step 1.

The ASPECT melt formulation solves for solid velocity, fluid pressure, and a separate compaction pressure `$p_c = (1-\varphi)(p_s-p_f) = -\xi\nabla\cdot\vec{u}_s$`, with its own equation, scaling, and preconditioner containing `$1/\xi$`. Zero permeability does not reduce it to our formulation: the limit is singular, and the code switches cells to non-melt, constrains `$p_c$` to zero, and yields plain single-phase Stokes with no bulk viscosity and no `$\Gamma$`. Melt models are stable because `$\Gamma$` is computed from the adiabatic pressure, their continuity is isentropic or incompressible (never `projected density field`), `$\Gamma$` is rate-limited with operator splitting, and the three-field system has a matching preconditioner. There is no pressure-to-rate loop for `$\xi$` to amplify.

Confirmed bugs: `Bexpl` writes `rhs_p`, a mass source in the continuity equation, with an alternating sign, a dimensionless `$\zeta/\eta$` coefficient, and a one-step-old divergence; `Bimpl` registers its `$\zeta$` term only in the Stokes system matrix, leaving the preconditioner and Newton assembler unchanged. Two `average()` methods are no-op TODOs. Bimpl's sign and `$4\eta/3$` factor are correct, the two formulations are never enabled together, and there is no year-to-second error. Unphysical math: a phase transformation changes reference density at fixed mass rather than producing viscous strain; with `$\zeta$` present the finite-element pressure differs from the mean normal stress by `$\zeta\nabla\cdot\vec{u}$` but `$\Delta G$` still uses the finite-element pressure; `$\bar{\beta}\partial\hat{P}/\partial t$` enters twice through an explicit lag; `Bexpl` violates mass conservation by construction. `Rel` and `Cap` work because they cut the feedback gain before `$\hat{P}$` reaches `$\Delta G$`.

Two precision findings. `DynpOff` is a double control: `project.mk:306-307` ties both density and Gibbs flags to the same switch, so it removes `$\hat{P}$` from density too, whereas the manuscript's stabilization removes only `$\hat{P}\Delta\bar{V}$` from `$\Delta G$`. The suite has no run that isolates the kinetic coupling alone. The ~100 MPa relaxed offset spans only 96.6–100.8 MPa across four orders of magnitude in `$\tau_p$`, so it is not a filter lag; two candidates are in the note.
